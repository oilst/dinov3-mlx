1 Why this explainer

Step 6 makes the MLX backbone fast. It then measures the speedup of mlx against torch-mps in every configuration of the benchmark matrix. This page explains the DINOv3 part of that work: how the cost of one image grows with the input resolution, and how the batch size changes the throughput. It also explains the measurement: the workers, the result lines, the speedup table with the pass rule of D21, and the GPU lock.

This page covers the functions of row 06-2-attention-and-batch-size.html in Table 5 of specs/06-speed/plan.html. Table 1 lists them. It also lists some public names of the same files that no row of Table 5 names. The line numbers are those of commit 2567808, the last commit of the Build stage. Each quote is an exact copy of the lines that its first line names. A quote marked "shortened" leaves out lines; "..." marks the gap.

The agent that wrote this page ran no test, no model and no GPU work. It read the numbers from the committed result files of Section 12 with a small CPU script. It computed the FLOPs of one image with the formula of flops.image_flops. It computed the reached TFLOP/s as throughput × GFLOP per image / 1000 (Section 7 of 03). All times are UTC on 2026-11-07.

The result in six numbers:

  • The quadratic term. For ViT-S/16 the SDPA share of the FLOPs grows from 7.9 % at 224 px to 63.7 % at 1024 px. But the median batch time grows only 30.91 times while the FLOPs grow 51.78 times (bf16-cast, batch size 8, one session). For ViT-H+/16 the time grows a little faster than the FLOPs from 512 to 1024 px: 5.48 against 5.07.
  • The GPU matmul limit. In bf16-cast at batch size 8, ViT-L/16 reaches about 29 TFLOP/s on both backends: mlx 26.7 to 28.0, torch-mps 29.4 to 29.9. Where both backends reach the same limit, the speedup stays near 1.
  • The Neural Accelerators. They give 1.87 to 1.88 times the throughput at P1 and 2.79 to 2.82 times at P2.
  • The batch size. ViT-S/16 at 224 px gives 22 % of its best throughput at batch size 1 and 82 % at batch size 8. From batch size 32 to 256 it stays within 2.4 % of the best. ViT-H+/16 at 512 px is flat within 8 % from batch size 1 to 16.
  • The pass rule D21. At P1, fp32 is above 1 (1.067); fp16 and bf16 are not above 1 (0.867 and 0.878). At P2 all three dtypes are above 1 (1.092, 1.032 and 1.013). In the matrix, 140 of 306 paired cells are above 1, 166 are not above 1, and 18 cells are empty by ADR 0008.
  • The GPU lock. All 941 lines of the six results/speed-*.jsonl files have an empty other_heavy_jobs_seen list: no heavy job outside the GPU lock ran before or after a worker.

2 Two terms of the cost

One square image of side s pixels gives T = 5 + (s / 16)² tokens: 1 CLS token, 4 register tokens and (s / 16)² patch tokens. part_flops of Step 3 counts the FLOPs of one block for B images:

# src/dinov3_mlx/flops.py, lines 73 to 81
    bt, d, f = batch_size * num_tokens, shape.dim, shape.ff
    ff_in_matrices = 2 if shape.gated else 1
    flops = dict.fromkeys(PART_NAMES, 0)
    flops["qkv_proj"] = 3 * 2 * bt * d * d
    flops["sdpa"] = 4 * batch_size * num_tokens * num_tokens * d
    flops["o_proj"] = 2 * bt * d * d
    flops["ff_in"] = ff_in_matrices * 2 * bt * d * f
    flops["ff_out"] = 2 * bt * f * d
    return flops
  • The linear term. qkv_proj, o_proj, ff_in and ff_out multiply each of the B · T token rows with a weight matrix. Their FLOPs are 2 · B · T · (4 D² + (k + 1) D F), with k = 2 for SwiGLU and k = 1 for the MLP. They grow as T.
  • The quadratic term. sdpa computes the scores Q Kᵀ (2 · T² · D for each image) and the weighted sum of V (again 2 · T² · D). Its FLOPs are 4 · B · T² · D. They grow as T².
  • The share. The ratio of the two terms is 2 T / (4 D + (k + 1) F) for each block, so the SDPA share grows with T and falls with D. For ViT-S/16 at 224 px it is 2 × 201 / (12 × 384) = 0.087. image_flops adds the patch embedding to the L blocks and returns the share.

The FLOP count is not the time. The SDPA kernel and the matmul kernels reach different rates, and the parts without FLOPs (the layer norms, RoPE, LayerScale, the activation and the casts) also take time. Section 3 gives the measured time.

The memory follows a different rule. A plain attention computes the full score matrix: B · heads · T² values for each block. required_bench_bytes of Step 3 counts this term for torch-mps. The MLX SDPA kernel (mx.fast.scaled_dot_product_attention) holds no T² buffer. The probe sdpa measured at most 0.012 MB of extra memory in all 139 cells, also in a cell where an fp32 score matrix would need 54.4 GB (Figure 3 of explore.html). So the mlx need has no score term (D8, Section 8.2).

Five facts follow from Tables 2 and 3:

  1. For ViT-S/16 the time grows less than the FLOPs. From 224 to 1024 px the FLOPs grow 51.78 times, but the median batch time grows only 30.91 times. The reached TFLOP/s rises from 14.4 to 24.1 in the Learn session. At 224 px the matmuls of ViT-S/16 are small (D = 384), and they reach a low rate. At 1024 px, 63.7 % of the FLOPs are in SDPA. The probe sdpa measured the fp16 SDPA kernel at the head shape of ViT-H+/16 (head dimension 64, the same as ViT-S/16; batch size 8) at 41.9 TFLOP/s for T = 1,029 and at about 57 TFLOP/s for T = 4,101 and more (Figure 3 of explore.html). These rows run in bf16-cast, and the bf16 kernel is slower at large T (R45, next item). So the fp16 rates are an upper limit here. But the measured rate of the whole forward pass still rises with T. So the quadratic term costs ViT-S/16 less time than its FLOPs predict.
  2. For ViT-H+/16 in bf16 the time grows a little more than the FLOPs. From 512 to 1024 px the FLOPs grow 5.07 times and the time 5.48 times. The reached TFLOP/s falls from 31.2 to 28.9. This agrees with R45: in bf16 the SDPA kernel is slower at large T, 35 TFLOP/s against 57 TFLOP/s in fp16 at T = 9,221 (probe sdpa). The Learn data has no fp16 row above 512 px, so it cannot compare fp16 and bf16 directly. In the matrix at T = 1,029, fp16-cast and bf16-cast of ViT-S/16 give about the same throughput (263.2 and 249.3 img/s at batch size 8, 265.3 and 266.2 img/s at batch size 32; Table 5 of results/speedup-table.md).
  3. At high input resolution, batch size 1 fills the GPU. ViT-S/16 in bf16-cast at 1024 px gives 48.0 img/s at batch size 1 and 49.5 img/s at batch size 8. ViT-H+/16 at 768 px gives 6.2 img/s at batch size 1 and 6.0 img/s at batch size 8. At 224 px the same ViT-S/16 row gives 524.5 img/s at batch size 1 and 1,893.0 img/s at batch size 8.
  4. No T² buffer. ViT-S/16 in fp32 at 1024 px and batch size 8 has an MLX peak of 1.97 GB. One fp32 score matrix of one block would hold 8 × 6 × 4,101² × 4 B = 3.23 GB. ViT-H+/16 in bf16-cast at 1024 px and batch size 8 has an MLX peak of 3.55 GB, with the weights. One bf16 score matrix of one block would hold 8 × 20 × 4,101² × 2 B = 5.38 GB. Each MLX peak is below one score matrix.
  5. The token range stays below the fp16 maximum. run_token_range_worker ran the eager token range of ViT-H+/16 in bf16-autocast on image 0. The largest value is 52,389.4 at 768 px and 52,784.1 at 1024 px (results/speed-learn.jsonl), below 65,504. At 512 px it was 52,312 (Table 9 of explore.html). So fp16-autocast is predicted to stay valid at high input resolution. Only a reference output above 512 px can confirm it.

5 Neural Accelerators on and off

The GPU of this Mac (Apple M5 Max, architecture applegpu_g17s) has Neural Accelerators: matrix-multiply units in each GPU core. MLX 0.32.4 uses them through the NAX kernels. def quantized_weight_bytes(model_size: str, bits: int, parameters: int | None = None) -> int:
    """Return the bytes of the weights of one model size with quantized block weights (Table 9 of
    specs/06-speed/plan.html).

    Each block weight takes bits / 8 bytes plus one fp16 or bf16 scale and one offset for each
    group of 64 weights. The other parameters take 2 bytes. The loader keeps the layer norms,
    LayerScale and the prefix tokens in fp32, so the value leaves out at most 2.0 MB (ViT-7B/16).

    Args:
        model_size: A key of MODEL_SHAPES.
        bits: 8 or 4.
        parameters: The parameter count. None reads it from the safetensors headers.

    Returns:
        block weights x (bits / 8 + 4 / 64) + the other parameters x 2.
    """
    shape = MODEL_SHAPES[model_size]
    count = _parameter_count(model_size) if parameters is None else parameters
    ff_matrices = 3 if shape.gated else 2
    block = shape.blocks * (4 * shape.dim**2 + ff_matrices * shape.dim * shape.ff)
    return block * (8 * bits + 4) // 64 + (count - block) * 2

9 The speedup table and the pass rule

The speedup of one configuration is the throughput of mlx divided by the throughput of the baseline row. ADR 0004 picks, on each side, the fastest valid row of each dtype and output. ADR 0009 widens it: the torch-mps side runs torch.compile in every valid dtype mode, in a paired group with the mlx workers. So each speedup divides by a row of the same session and power state, a few minutes apart.

def pass_verdict(run_medians: list[float], pooled_median: float) -> str:
    """Return the verdict of a speedup at P1 or P2 (D21, option b).

    "above 1" only if the pooled median of all paired ratios and the median of each run are above
    1. Two runs that disagree in sign give "not above 1". A pooled median within 1 ± NOISE gets the
    mark "inside the noise" (Section 3.15 of the plan; finding V5 of verify.html).
    """
    above = pooled_median > 1 and all(median > 1 for median in run_medians)
    verdict = "above 1" if above else "not above 1"
    return f"{verdict}, inside the noise" if abs(pooled_median - 1) <= NOISE else verdict


def single_verdict(ratio: float) -> str:

    verdict = "above 1" if ratio > 1 else "not above 1"
    return f"{verdict}, inside the noise" if abs(ratio - 1) <= NOISE else verdict


def spread_between_runs(run_medians: list[float]) -> float:
    """Return (largest run median − smallest run median) / smallest run median."""

            median = _lower_median([_ips(other, output) for other in eager[key]])
            if _ips(line, output) < median:
                listed.add(key)
    order = {size: index for index, size in enumerate(MODEL_SIZES)}
    return sorted(listed, key=lambda key: (order[key[0]], *key[1:]))

def other_heavy_jobs(pattern: str = HEAVY_JOB_PATTERN, registry: Path = LOCK_REGISTRY) -> list[str]:
    """Return the command lines of the heavy jobs outside the GPU lock.

    A process outside the lock matches pattern and is none of these: the own process or one of its
    ancestors, a process of the own process group (for example the other commands of the shell
    pipeline that started this job), a registered process, an ancestor or a child of a registered
    process.

    Args:
        pattern: The regular expression of the command lines of heavy jobs.
        registry: The folder of the registry entries.

    Returns:
        The command lines, sorted by process id.
    """
    groups = _process_groups()
    table = {pid: (ppid, command) for pid, (ppid, _, command) in groups.items()}
    own_group = os.getpgid(0)
    registered = registered_processes(registry, table)
    known = _ancestors(os.getpid(), table) | _descendants(registered, table)
    for pid in registered:
        known |= _ancestors(pid, table)
    regex = re.compile(pattern)
    return [
        command
        for pid, (_, command) in sorted(table.items())
        if pid not in known and groups[pid][1] != own_group and regex.search(command)
    ]
  • Why leave out the ancestors. A process that waits inside heavy_job matches the pattern, and so does its uv run parent. Without line 136 each job would wait for itself.
  • Why leave out the registered processes. Two lock-aware waiters both match the pattern. If each waited for all matching processes before the flock, they would wait for each other for ever. The registry marks them as lock-aware; the flock sets their order.
  • Why leave out the own process group (line 143). A shell pipeline such as uv run pytest | tee log starts its commands in one process group. They are not ancestors of each other.

11 Surprises

  1. The quadratic term makes ViT-S/16 more efficient, not less. From 224 to 1024 px its FLOPs grow 51.78 times, but its batch time grows only 30.91 times (Table 3). The SDPA kernel at large T runs at a higher rate than the small matmuls of D = 384 (probe sdpa, measured in fp16; the measured bf16 rows agree, Figure 2). The SDPA share of 63.7 % at 1024 px (Table 4 of the plan) is a share of FLOPs, not of time.
  2. No T² buffer in the MLX SDPA kernel. The probe sdpa found at most 0.012 MB of extra memory in 139 cells. The Learn data agrees: each MLX peak at 1024 px is below the size of one score matrix of one block (1.97 GB against 3.23 GB, 3.55 GB against 5.38 GB). So required_bench_bytes drops the score term on mlx, and at P2 the two needs differ by exactly that term.
  3. bf16 SDPA is slower at large T. The probe sdpa measured 35 TFLOP/s in bf16 against 57 TFLOP/s in fp16 at T = 9,221 (R45). In one session, ViT-H+/16 in bf16-cast needs 5.48 times the time for 5.07 times the FLOPs from 512 to 1024 px, and its reached TFLOP/s falls from 31.2 to 28.9. The Learn data has no fp16 row above 512 px, so it shows the effect only against the FLOPs, not against fp16.
  4. ViT-S/16 at 224 px is best at batch size 32 or more; ViT-H+/16 at 512 px is flat. The probe batch-knee found the best ViT-S/16 batch size at 32 (eager). The compiled sweep gives 64 in round 1, with 32 to 256 within 2.4 %, and again 64 among the later workers of round 2. ViT-H+/16 stays within 8 % from batch size 1 to 16. At 768 and 1024 px batch size 1 already fills the GPU.
  5. The Mac lost up to 30 % of its throughput within five minutes. Round 2 of the sweep ran 4.6 to 30.1 % slower than round 1, with AC power, no thermal warning and no job outside the GPU lock. The two workers of ViT-S/16 at batch size 128 started 4.5 min apart (19:20:09 and 19:24:36 UTC). The mean of the two rounds in Table 6 of results/speedup-table.md therefore has its highest value at batch size 256 for ViT-S/16. A single sweep round in a rotated order cannot separate the batch size from such a drift; the paired rounds of the speedup runs can.
  6. torch.compile gains 36 to 71 % outside P1 and P2. The probe torch-compile-offp measured it. The committed lines give 1.41 to 2.23 across days and 1.13 to 1.30 in one session (Section 9.3). For ViT-7B/16 at 512 px and batch size 8 the gain is 1.00. A compiled mlx row against an eager torch-mps row would overstate the speedup by about this factor, so every torch-mps worker of the matrix runs torch.compile (ADR 0009).
  7. The compile check found only the drift between sessions. Its four groups (all ViT-S/16, fp32) had a compiled matrix row below the Step 3 eager median. In the follow-up groups the compiled row was 13 to 30 % faster than the eager row of the same session.
  8. The spread between runs is large for the throughput and small for the paired ratio. Step 5 measured about 20 % between runs on different days, against 1.6 to 3.0 % of noise inside one session. In Step 6 the raw throughput at P2 moved by 8.3 to 20.9 % between two runs 5.5 h apart, but the paired ratios moved by only 0.4 to 4.1 % (Table 10). The pairing turns the large term into the small one.
  9. The Neural Accelerators give 1.87 to 2.82 times the throughput. The build measured more than the probe nax-ab (1.81 and 2.69). Without them mlx reaches 11.1 TFLOP/s at P2, far below the 27.8 TFLOP/s of torch-mps. So torch-mps very probably also uses the matrix units, and the matmul limit of Section 4 is then the same hardware for both backends. The result files do not record the kernels of torch-mps, so this is a conclusion from the rates only.
  10. At P1 Python is not the problem. The host share is 3.3 to 3.5 %. One compiled forward pass of ViT-S/16 has 637 operation nodes (841 eager), about 6 µs of GPU time each. The profile cannot name the part where torch.compile wins, because it has no torch.compile part times.
  11. PAIR_FIELDS is not used. The driver writes the pairing fields from its own list PAIR_KEYS. A change of one list does not change the other.
