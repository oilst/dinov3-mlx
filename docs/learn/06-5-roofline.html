1 Why this explainer

The speedup table of Step 6 gives the throughput of the compiled MLX port. It does not tell how close each part of a block is to the limits of this Mac. This page puts each part of block 1 of ViT-H+/16 at 512 px (T = 1,029 tokens), batch size 8, on a roofline. The configuration is P2 of specs/06-speed/plan.html, in fp16-cast and bf16-cast. The Mac is an Apple M5 Max with 40 GPU cores and 64 GB.

2 Arithmetic intensity and the roofline

The arithmetic intensity I of a part is its FLOPs divided by the bytes that it moves between memory and the GPU. Its unit is FLOP/B.

attainable rate = min(matmul roof, memory roof × I)

A part with a low intensity waits for memory: it is memory-bound, and its limit is the slanted roof. A part with a high intensity waits for the arithmetic units: it is compute-bound, and its limit is the flat roof. The ridge point is the intensity where the two roofs meet: matmul roof / memory roof.

4 The roofline of one block

  • The five matmul parts have an intensity of 514 to 1,000 FLOP/B: 4.6 to 8.9 times the ridge point. They are compute-bound. They reach 60 to 85 % of the matmul roof.
  • The six element-wise parts have an intensity of 0.33 to 2 FLOP/B. They are memory-bound. Timed alone, they reach 28 to 71 % of the memory roof.
  • Block 1 as one compiled function reaches 39.8 TFLOP/s in fp16-cast: 67 % of the fp16 matmul roof. The forward pass gives 20.6 img/s × 1,902 GFLOP per image (flops.image_flops) = 39.3 TFLOP/s.
  • fp16-cast and bf16-cast are almost equal. bf16-cast needs 10 % more time in ff_in and 8 % more time in ff_out.

6 What the roofline says about fusion

Fusion joins kernels, so that intermediate values stay on the GPU and do not go to memory. Thus fusion helps a memory-bound part. It does not raise the rate of a compute-bound part. The roofline gives an upper limit for each kind of fusion.

  • Element-wise fusion. In fp16-cast the five matmul parts take 10.42 ms. Block 1 takes 11.93 ms. So the layer norms and the fused element-wise kernels take about 1.51 ms inside the block: 13 % of the block time. At the memory roof the element-wise bytes need 1.12 ms. A fusion of these parts into the matmul kernels (an epilogue) can save at most the 13 %. mx.compile already fuses the element-wise chains (06-1). The layer norms stay their own kernels. An epilogue fusion needs a custom Metal kernel that is as fast as the matmul kernel of MLX.
  • Projection fusion (D6 b). This fusion does not reduce the bytes. It gives fewer and larger matmuls, and on this Mac a larger matmul reaches a higher rate (Table 1). Table 5 gives the estimate at P2.
  • The matmul parts. At the fp16 matmul roof the five matmul parts would take 8.02 ms in place of 10.42 ms. This gap is in the matmul and SDPA kernels of MLX, not in the port.

7 The joined projections, measured

Step 6e built the projection fusion of Table 5 as the joined projection and measured it in a paired run (change C7 of specs/06e-kernels/plan.html). Figure 3 shows the separate form R and the joined projection of q, k and v. The joined projection of gate and up has the same structure, with two weights.

  • set_joined_projections(model, qkv, ff) sets the plain attribute joined of each Attention and SwiGLU module. A plain attribute is not a parameter. So load_weights, set_dtype and nn.quantize do not see it. A trace reads the attribute when it records, so call the function before compile_backbone. The function raises ValueError for a model with quantized weights, because packed weights, scales and offsets need their own join.
  • _joined(layers, x) joins the weights with mx.concatenate inside the trace, at each call. The weights stay inputs of the trace. So a weight change reaches the output, and no second copy of the weights stays in memory between calls.
  • _joined_bias(layers) joins the biases. A layer without a bias gets a bias of zeros. ViT-H+/16 has a bias on q_proj and v_proj, but not on k_proj.
  • joined_linear(x, weight, bias) makes the same calls as nn.Linear: mx.addmm with a bias, else a matmul.
  • Attention.qkv splits the joined output (B, T, 3D) into q, k and v with one reshape and one transpose. SwiGLU.hidden splits its joined output (B, T, 2F) with mx.split.

  • Equal outputs. In all rows, the first batch of each joined form is equal to R, bit for bit.
  • Slower at P2. At P2 with batch size 8, R-Jqkv gives 0.952 to 0.961, R-Jff 0.989 to 0.997 and R-J 0.940 to 0.954. No join reaches the limit 1.03 of D6. So both joins stay off in every dtype mode, and the compiled path of the package does not change.
  • More memory. The joined forms need up to 546 MB more MLX peak memory than R.
  • A cost outside the graph at P1. ViT-S/16 has no gate. So at P1 the form R-Jff runs the same graph as R. Its ratio is still below 1, because the harness switches the flags before and after each batch (_JoinedForm in mlx_bench.py): set_joined_projections visits each block and checks the model for quantized weights. This costs 0.15 ms per batch: 4 % of a P1 batch of 4.0 ms. All joined forms pay this cost at P1, so the P1 rows of Table 6 read low by about that share. At P2 a batch takes at least 388 ms, so the same cost stays below 0.1 %.

Why is the joined form slower at P2? Table 1 shows that one 1,280 → 3,840 matmul is faster than three 1,280 → 1,280 matmuls, and Table 5 estimates a saving of 8.5 % of the block time in fp16-cast. The concat of the weights of both joins moves 136.6 µs of bytes per block at the memory roof: 1.14 % of block 1. These effects predict a gain, not a loss of 4 to 5 % for R-Jqkv. The paired run measures only the whole forward pass. It does not show where the joined form loses the time. A profile of the parts of the joined form can show it.

8 Other tile sizes in a source build

A matmul kernel computes its output in tiles. One threadgroup computes one tile of bm × bn output values and steps through the sum over K in parts of bk. wm × wn simdgroups share the tile. The attention kernel uses tiles of bq queries and bk keys. MLX selects the tile size from the device and the shape. MLX 0.32.4 does this in steel_matmul_regular_axpby_nax (mlx/backend/metal/matmul.cpp) and in sdpa_full_self_attention_nax (mlx/backend/metal/scaled_dot_product_attention.cpp).

The probe C1 of Step 6e built MLX 0.32.4 from its source code, outside the repo, in its own venv (ADR 0013). A patch reads a tile size from an environment variable at each dispatch (the tile override) and adds the kernels of the other tile sizes. The package keeps the released MLX.

  • Control. The unchanged source build has the speed of the wheel: 14 of 14 shapes are within ± 3 % (Table 1 of results/kernels-c1.md). So a ratio in the source build is also a ratio for the wheel.
  • Noise. Each target ran 12 tile sizes. One of them is the choice of MLX, the same tile size as the control. Its ratio to the control is within ± 0.7 %. This is the noise of one sweep.
  • Wrong outputs. 3 attention tile sizes with bk = 16 run without an error, but give wrong outputs (smallest row cosine -0.28). MLX does not ship these tile sizes; the patch added them. Only the cosine check found them.
  • Effect on the block. T1 and T3 are shapes of the default form. With the best tile sizes, qkv_proj, o_proj and sdpa of block 1 in fp16-cast would save about 0.10 ms of 11.93 ms: 0.8 %. This is below the noise of one paired run (V17 of Step 6). T2 is the joined gate and up, which D6 keeps off.

9 How the page counts FLOPs and bytes

  • The FLOPs of the matmul parts come from flops.part_flops. One multiply-add counts as 2 FLOPs. SDPA counts 4 B T² D.
  • The bytes come from flops.part_bytes with 2 bytes for each value: each part reads its inputs and its weights once and writes its outputs once. A real kernel can move more bytes, so the true intensity can be lower.
  • flops.py counts 0 FLOPs for the element-wise parts. A point with 0 FLOPs has no position on a log axis. So roofline_page.elementwise_flops uses an estimate for each value: layer norm 8, RoPE 6 (3 on q and 3 on k), LayerScale with the residual add 2, SwiGLU activation 6. The estimate moves only the six element-wise points. Their GB/s does not change.
  • At batch size 1 the linear layers have a lower intensity: qkv_proj 497, o_proj 395, ff_in 540 and ff_out 513 FLOP/B. They are still above the ridge point. SDPA stays at 514 FLOP/B.

10 How we measured

  • Part times. scripts/bench_speed.py profile --priority P2 runs mlx_bench.run_profile_worker in one subprocess for each dtype mode, through the GPU lock, on AC power (results/speed-profile-p2.jsonl). Each part runs alone and compiled: 3 warm-up calls, then the median of 20 calls with one mx.eval after each call.
  • The fixed cost of each call. At P1 each part takes at least about 0.17 ms, also the smallest part. So the element-wise times of this page include a fixed cost, and their GB/s values are lower limits.
  • Roofs. scripts/probe_roofs.py with MLX_ENABLE_TF32=0 (results/roofs.jsonl). The values of this page come from the first form of the probe: two scripts with the same probes. That run did not record the machine state (field note).
  • The plain a @ b reached only 26.1 to 41.4 TFLOP/s in fp16, and 8.7 to 14.4 TFLOP/s in fp32.
  • Joined projections (Section 7). scripts/bench_speed.py change --change joined runs the four forms R, R-J, R-Jqkv and R-Jff in one worker process for each configuration (mlx_bench.run_change_worker). change_forms gives each joined form its own compiled forward pass, which _JoinedForm wraps: it sets the flags with set_joined_projections before each batch and clears them after it. run_forms runs 5 rounds; the order of the forms rotates by one from round to round, and each form gets its own warm-up and timed batches in each round. The ratio of a form is its throughput ÷ the throughput of R in the same round. first_batch_check compares the first batch of each form with R, bit for bit, and records the largest differences. Two runs at different times give results/speed-joined.jsonl.
  • Tile sizes (Section 8). scripts/probe_kernels.py starts scripts/probe_kernels_worker.py with python -I in the venv of the wheel or of the source build, one worker at a time, through the GPU lock. Each probe time is the median of 20 calls with one mx.eval after each call. The control alternates the two venvs for 5 rounds; the sweep compares each tile size with the control build (results/kernels-c1.jsonl).

11 Surprises

  • The first matmul roof came from a square 8,192 matmul. It was lower than the rate of the ff_in part. The linear layout, with the weight stored as (out, in), is faster on this Mac than a @ b. So the roof must come from the same layout as the model.
  • In bf16 the fused 1,280 → 10,240 layer is not faster than two 1,280 → 5,120 layers (Table 1). In fp16 it is.
  • The compiled block is faster than the sum of its parts. A part alone pays a fixed cost and writes its output to memory; inside the block the element-wise chains are fused.
  • The joined projection is slower than R at P2, although its one matmul is faster than the separate matmuls alone (Sections 6 and 7).
  • At P1 one Python call for each batch costs about 4 % of the throughput (Section 7). A harness that switches a form at each call must count this cost.
  • 3 attention tile sizes give wrong outputs without an error (Section 8).
