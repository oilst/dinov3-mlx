Step 6b Prior-art comparison: Plan

Roadmap step 6b. Status: draft for approval. Decisions: D1 to D9 of explore.html, recommended options (the user asked the agent to decide, night of 2026-11-07). Date: 2026-11-07.

1 Goal

Step 6b measures the related public repos with our harness. It gives a prior-art ratio (our throughput divided by the throughput of the other repo, in the same round) and the parity of each Core ML export at its own model size, input resolution and batch sizes 1 and 8. It measures the cost in parity and speed of each port change in our own code, because the permission system refuses to run the two Python MLX ports. The ports run later with one command if the user allows it (question Q1).

The Explore probes show the expected result: on the GPU the Core ML exports and our compiled forward pass are equal within 6 % at 448 and 512 px, and ours is 1.57 times faster at 224 px with batch size 8 (Table 4 of explore.html). With float input every export reproduces the reference output; the 8-bit image input and the Neural Engine cost parity.

2 DINOv3 topic

Which parts of the backbone each port changes, and what that costs in parity. The port changes are the RoPE in bf16 (periods, tables and the rotation of q and k), the Meta RoPE periods, the unfused attention, the fused qkv projection with its k-bias mask, the RoPE tables in each block, and the 8-bit image input of the Core ML exports. The Learn stage writes the new explainer docs/learn/06b-prior-ports-measured.html: Table 2 of explore.html, the parity of the port-change variants V1 to V5, their speed, and the Core ML rows. It links to 01-prior-ports.html and 04-2-rope.html.

3.5 Workers and result lines

  • Ours (J1, J5): run_mlx_worker of mlx_bench.py with config.compile=True, the time budgets WARM_UP and TIMED, and batch= random pixels for 592 px. The driver sets MLX_ENABLE_TF32=0 in the environment of every worker of ours (R4 of explore.html).
  • Core ML (J1, J2): the spec JSON gives the package path, the input kind, the compute units, the batch size, the time budgets and the output file. The worker loads the export (for the spec-array input: the input feature of the loaded spec becomes a FLOAT32 multiArray (1, 3, H, W); the package on disk does not change). It predicts the 3 test images at batch size 1 and writes their outputs. Then it builds the batch inputs before the timing, warms up and times predict calls with the budgets. It prints {"status", "load_s", "warm_up_batches", "timed_batches", "times_s", "throughput_ips", "outputs_file", "coremltools"}.
  • Variants (J3, J4): load_mlx_backbone, then variant_forward. Parity: the 12 configurations, measure_parity, pass or fail against THRESHOLDS[mode]. Speed: time_batch_mlx and phase of mlx_bench.py with the budgets.
  • Lines in results/prior-art.jsonl: run "P6b", job, round, position, group, side_kind ("ours", "export", "variant", "port"), candidate, export, variant, model_size, side, batch_size, dtype_mode or precision, units, input, status, reason, patch and frame (throughput_ips, median_s), parity (each test image and the worst), valid, load_s, the memory-check fields, other_heavy_jobs_seen, machine.

3.6 Parity of an export

export_parity(reference, frame, patches) follows check_validity: it builds the token sequence from the frame embedding (the reference CLS row when the export has no frame embedding), the 4 reference register rows and the patch embeddings, and calls measure_parity. Without a frame embedding the line stores frame: null. The cosine does not depend on the length of a vector, so the L2-normalized outputs of C7 and C8 need no change. A grid output (1, D, h, w) becomes (h · w, D) in row-major order. The verdict uses THRESHOLDS["fp32"] for FP32 and INT8 exports and THRESHOLDS["fp16-cast"] for FP16 exports (ADR 0003); INT8 is report only.

3.7 Rows and the table

prior_art_rows(lines) pairs each export line with the line of ours of the same round, group and batch size, and gives one row for each cell: the 5 ratios (ours ÷ export, patch output), their lower median, the spread (largest − smallest) ÷ smallest, single_verdict of the median, the median throughput of both sides, and the worst parity of the export. variant_rows gives the parity rows of J3 and the change ratios of J4 (V1 ÷ V0, V4 ÷ V0; the lower median of 3). bench_prior_art.py table writes results/prior-art-table.md: 1 the candidates (C1 to C9), 2 the Core ML cells, 3 the image-input and ALL rows, 4 the variant parity, 5 the variant speed, 6 the port cells or their "blocked" rows.

3.8 Run rules and third-party rules

  • One GPU lock for the driver; one worker at a time in its own subprocess (run_worker_subprocess of bench.py, timeout 900 s).
  • Memory need: ours and variants: required_bench_bytes with backend="mlx"; an export: 3 × the size of its weight.bin + 2 GB; a port worker: as ours, and the worker sets mx.set_memory_limit. A worker that does not fit gives a "skipped" line.
  • The driver starts a third-party worker only with the interpreter of its venv and -I, and refuses a worker path inside a candidate folder.
  • No third-party package in pyproject.toml or uv.lock. No candidate file in the repo. No pickle load in a worker.
  • The driver refuses to start when the venv of a job is missing, with the path in the message.

3.9 ADRs of the Close stage

  • ADR 0012: the prior-art ratio. Our compiled forward pass against each export at the export's own configuration, 5 paired rounds, the lower median, the spec-array input for the parity of an image-input export.
  • ADR 0013: third-party code. Each candidate lives in its own folder outside the repo with its own venv; Python runs with -I; no pickle from a third party; a run of third-party Python code needs the user's permission.

def load_export(path: Path, input_kind: str, units: str) -> "ct.models.MLModel":
    import coremltools as ct

    unit = getattr(ct.ComputeUnit, units)
    if input_kind != "spec-array":
        return ct.models.MLModel(str(path), compute_units=unit)
    spec = ct.utils.load_spec(str(path))
    feature = spec.description.input[0]  # imageType (H, W, RGB) in the package
    height, width = feature.type.imageType.height, feature.type.imageType.width
    feature.type.multiArrayType.shape[:] = [1, 3, height, width]  # the oneof moves to multiArray
    feature.type.multiArrayType.dataType = ct.proto.FeatureTypes_pb2.ArrayFeatureType.FLOAT32
    weights = path / "Data" / "com.apple.CoreML" / "weights"
    return ct.models.MLModel(spec, weights_dir=str(weights), compute_units=unit)

# src/dinov3_apple/prior_art.py: the RoPE of C1 and C2 (V1), as the port computes it
def port_rope_tables(grid_h: int, grid_w: int, head_dim: int, base: float) -> tuple[mx.array, mx.array]:
    """Return (cos, sin), each (Hp · Wp, head_dim) in bf16, with the steps of the Meta-based ports.

    The ports return (sin, cos); this function returns our order, because Block passes (cos, sin).
    """
    bf16 = mx.bfloat16
    periods = base ** (2 * mx.arange(head_dim // 4, dtype=bf16) / (head_dim // 2))
    coords_h = mx.arange(0.5, grid_h, dtype=bf16) / grid_h
    coords_w = mx.arange(0.5, grid_w, dtype=bf16) / grid_w
    mesh = mx.stack(mx.meshgrid(coords_h, coords_w, indexing="ij"), axis=-1).reshape(-1, 2)
    angles = 2 * math.pi * (2.0 * mesh - 1.0)[:, :, None] / periods[None, None, :]
    angles = mx.tile(angles.reshape(angles.shape[0], -1), (1, 2))
    return mx.cos(angles), mx.sin(angles)


class Bf16RotationAttention(Attention):
    """Attention with the rotation of the Meta-based ports: q and k go to bf16, all tokens.

    cos and sin are the tables of port_rope_tables (patch tokens only); the 5 prefix tokens keep
    their bf16 values without a rotation, as in the ports.
    """

    def __call__(self, x: mx.array, cos: mx.array, sin: mx.array) -> mx.array:
        q, k, v = self.qkv(x)
        q, k = (port_rotate(t.astype(mx.bfloat16), cos, sin).astype(t.dtype) for t in (q, k))
        a = mx.fast.scaled_dot_product_attention(q, k, v, scale=self.scale, mask=None)
        return self.output(a)
# src/dinov3_apple/prior_art.py: one row of the Core ML pairs (D6)
def cell_row(ours: list[dict], export: list[dict]) -> PriorArtRow:
    by_round = {line["round"]: line for line in ours if line["status"] == "done"}
    ratios = [
        by_round[line["round"]]["patch"]["throughput_ips"] / line["patch"]["throughput_ips"]
        for line in export
        if line["status"] == "done" and line["round"] in by_round
    ]
    median = _lower_median(ratios)
    return PriorArtRow(ratios=ratios, median=median, spread=(max(ratios) - min(ratios)) / min(ratios),
                       verdict=single_verdict(median), ...)

6 Tests

Each test goes red first, then green. The default session needs no gated weights, no coremltools and no third-party code. T13 to T16 run in the slow session.

  1. test_candidates_cover_the_explore_table (T1): CANDIDATES has C1 to C9 with license, commit and verdict; each "skip" and "gated" verdict has a reason.
  2. test_exports_have_package_paths_inside_prior_art_dir (T2): E1 to E7 point under PRIOR_ART_DIR; the token count of E7 and of our 592 px cell are both 1374.
  3. test_export_parity_patch_only_and_normalized (T3): an export output equal to the reference patches times a positive factor for each token gives patch parity 1.0 and frame null; a grid (1, D, h, w) maps to row-major (h · w, D).
  4. test_coreml_pair_plan (T4): 5 rounds × 24 workers; ours first in odd rounds and last in even rounds; E1 and E2 share the worker of ours, E4 and E5 too; the group order rotates.
  5. test_cell_row_ratio_and_verdict (T5): the ratio is ours ÷ export; the lower median; the spread; single_verdict with the noise mark; a failed round drops only its pair.
  6. test_port_rope_tables_match_a_numpy_oracle (T6): port_rope_tables equals an oracle that rounds to bf16 after each step (NumPy, bf16 by bit truncation with round-to-nearest-even), and differs from the fp32 tables by more than 0 and at most 0.02 in sin and cos.
  7. test_meta_periods_angle_difference (T7): the V3 tables differ from the Hugging Face angles by 0.00791 rad (14 × 14 grid) and 0.00825 rad (32 × 32 grid), within 1e-5 (the values of T9 in test_mlx_rope.py).
  8. test_variant_v0_is_bit_for_bit (T8): on the small model, variant_forward(model, "V0") equals compile_backbone(model) with largest difference 0.0.
  9. test_unfused_attention_matches_sdpa (T9): on the small model in fp32, V4 differs from V0 by at most 1e-5; in fp16-cast by at most the fp16 fault bound of Step 6 (0.03).
  10. test_bf16_rotation_rounds_prefix_tokens (T10): Bf16RotationAttention on the small model differs from V0 by more than 0; the q of a prefix token is the bf16 value of the V0 q.
  11. test_eight_bit_pixels (T11): the round trip moves each pixel by at most 0.5 / 255 / std of its channel; pixels on the 8-bit grid stay the same.
  12. test_workers_are_standalone_and_safe (T12): an AST scan of the two worker files: imports only from the standard library, numpy, safetensors, coremltools, PIL (Core ML worker) or mlx (port worker); no dinov3_apple; no pickle, torch.load, subprocess with a shell, eval or exec. pyproject.toml and uv.lock name no coremltools, mlxim or mlx-image.
  13. test_ports_subcommand_is_gated (T13, default session): without --allow-third-party-python, ports writes 8 lines with the status "blocked" and the command, and starts no subprocess (a fake runner).
  14. test_driver_env_and_command (T14): the worker of ours gets MLX_ENABLE_TF32=0; a third-party worker gets the venv interpreter and -I; a missing venv stops the driver before the lock.
  15. test_coreml_worker_end_to_end (T15, slow, skip without venv-coreml): E1 at batch size 1 with a 1 s budget: status "done", 3 outputs, patch parity of the 3 test images ≥ 0.9999 (fp32 thresholds).
  16. test_variant_parity_vits16 (T16, slow): V0 on ViT-S/16 fp32 at 224 px equals the Step 5 parity bit for bit; V1 gives a lower patch 1st percentile than V0.
